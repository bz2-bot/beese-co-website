# 1. The Core Mission

Beese & Co. is not building a decorative brochure website.

The website exists to make it unbelievably easy for a customer to confidently place a **complete custom order** and give Beese & Co. money with as little back-and-forth messaging as possible.

Every page, feature, product option, photo, heading, form field, and technical decision must support at least one of these outcomes:

1. Help customers understand what they can order.
2. Help customers choose the correct product and customization method.
3. Collect complete and usable order information upfront.
4. Reduce repeated questions, missing artwork, and manual follow-up.
5. Build trust before the customer reaches checkout.
6. Improve search visibility and bring new qualified customers to Beese & Co.
7. Support profitable growth without creating operational chaos.

A beautiful website that creates more work for Dana is a failure.

A practical website that converts, collects the correct information, and makes the business easier to run is the goal.

# 2. Brand Positioning

## Who Beese & Co. Is

Beese & Co. is an established, eight-year custom product business based in Spring Grove, Illinois.

The brand should feel:

- handcrafted
- trustworthy
- capable
- personal
- polished
- modern but timeless
- premium without feeling corporate
- approachable without looking homemade or amateur
- locally rooted but able to serve customers beyond the local area

Beese & Co. combines custom UV printing, laser engraving, thoughtful design, and practical customer service.

## What Beese & Co. Is Not

Beese & Co. is not:

- a generic sign shop
- a disposable craft marketplace brand
- a trendy template storefront
- a faceless print-on-demand business
- a bargain personalization shop
- a website full of vague inspiration but no clear ordering path
- a corporate promotional-products catalog

The closest acceptable sign-related offering is intentional laser-cut work. Traditional sign-shop jobs should not become the core business.

## Brand Promise

Beese & Co. helps customers turn their idea, logo, memory, gift, organization, school, bookstore, business, or event into a well-made custom product—with a clear proof process before production.

## Internal Mission

Build a thriving, profitable business that lifts others up along the way.

# 3. Strategic Business Direction

## The Website Must Serve Three Revenue Lanes

### 1. Retail Custom Orders

Customers ordering one or a small number of personalized products.

This lane needs:

- clear product choices
- simple personalization
- artwork upload
- special instructions
- transparent proof expectations
- confidence before checkout

### 2. Bulk and Wholesale Orders

Bookstores, schools, teams, local businesses, organizations, trophy shops, production partners, and repeat buyers.

This lane should emphasize:

- dependable production
- repeat ordering
- volume-friendly products
- simple quote or bulk-order entry points
- logo and artwork guidance
- realistic lead-time expectations
- scalable products rather than chaotic one-off requests

Bookstores are a particularly strong strategic fit for premium engraved tumblers and Beese & Co.'s developing bookish artwork collections.

### 3. Specialty and Seasonal Products

Ornaments, golf products, memorial items, bookstore merchandise, gifts, and future focused collections.

These should be built as intentional product systems, not random additions.

## Growth Principle

The business should grow in ways that do not break production.

Prioritize:

- products Dana can make consistently
- healthy margins
- repeatable options
- easy-to-understand customer choices
- repeat wholesale and bulk buyers
- fewer incomplete or confusing orders
- fewer low-value custom conversations

Do not chase every possible custom-product category.

# 4. Customer Experience North Star

The customer should never have to guess:

- what Beese & Co. sells
- how to begin
- which customization method they need
- what file to upload
- whether they will receive a proof
- what happens after checkout
- how to request a bulk order
- how to return to another tumbler size or product path

## The Ideal Order Journey

1. Customer arrives through search, social media, a direct link, or a recommendation.
2. Customer immediately understands the relevant product or category.
3. Customer sees clear, non-overwhelming choices.
4. Customer chooses product, size, color, and decoration method.
5. Customer enters personalization and uploads artwork when applicable.
6. Customer acknowledges the proof process.
7. Customer submits all needed instructions before checkout.
8. Beese & Co. reviews the order and artwork.
9. Customer receives a proof by text before production.
10. Customer approves.
11. Beese & Co. produces, packs, and ships or prepares pickup/delivery.

## Locked Proof Language

Use this wording or a carefully improved version that preserves the full meaning:

> I understand I will receive a proof by text before production. Please make sure my phone number is correct.

The proof process is a trust feature and an operational requirement. It should be visible, understandable, and never buried.

# 5. Design Vibe and Visual Direction

## Overall Feel

The site should feel like a premium, independent custom goods shop—not a mass-market catalog and not a craft template.

The visual language should combine:

- black
- warm cream or off-white space
- restrained gold accents
- natural wood tones
- strong product photography
- clean typography
- generous but not wasteful spacing
- intentional image boxes and product groupings

## Design Personality

The website should be:

- confident
- warm
- grounded
- capable
- clean
- useful
- product-first
- visually rich without being cluttered

Avoid:

- oversized empty hero sections
- excessive zoomed photography
- trendy decorative clutter
- generic Shopify-demo styling
- endless text before products
- excessive rounded cards without purpose
- loud gradients
- fake luxury
- overly feminine craft clichés
- corporate coldness

## Photography Rules — Locked

1. Never crop or zoom an image simply to force it into a section.
2. Product and hero images must show the full important subject.
3. Use `object-fit: contain`, cream space, a better frame, or a different photograph when needed.
4. Do not sacrifice the product for edge-to-edge visual filling.
5. Product images should use a consistent, approximately square, YETI-like presentation ratio where practical.
6. Photo placeholders are acceptable during production and must be easy to replace.
7. The site must not be held back while waiting for perfect lifestyle photography.
8. Image boxes are preferred over empty sections containing only headings and text.

## Hero Rules — Locked

- Heroes must not dominate the entire first screen.
- Heroes must not use giant zoomed-in product crops.
- The customer should reach useful shopping choices quickly.
- Hero copy should be concise.
- The purpose of the page must be obvious without a long brand manifesto.

## Typography

Preserve the established Beese & Co. visual direction and logo relationship.

Do not casually replace fonts or alter typography that has already been approved. Typography changes require a measurable usability, performance, or brand improvement—not personal preference.

# 8. Tumbler Strategy

Tumblers are a flagship category and must be built as a coherent buying system.

## Tumbler Shopping Principle

The customer should begin with a clear question:

> How would you like to shop?

Possible paths may include:

- by tumbler size
- laser engraved
- full color printed
- custom logo or artwork
- bulk orders

The exact presentation may evolve, but reducing confusion and unnecessary clicks is more important than preserving a rigid old funnel.

## Product Family Direction

Primary drinkware categories include:

- 20 oz tumblers
- 22 oz skinny tumblers
- 30 oz tumblers
- 40 oz tumblers
- water bottles in applicable sizes
- handled styles
- sports styles
- laser engraving
- full color printing where technically and aesthetically appropriate

## Master Product Template Principle

Use a reusable product architecture with only the options that apply to that product.

Potential fields include:

- size
- color
- quantity
- personalization
- decoration method
- artwork upload
- special instructions
- proof acknowledgment
- pickup/shipping information where relevant

Do not show irrelevant fields merely because another product uses them.

## 22 oz Master Product — Locked Current Pricing

- Single-sided: **$28**
- Double-sided: **$32**
- Full wrap: **$45**

## 22 oz Decoration Rules — Locked

- Laser engraving is the preferred method for full wraps.
- Full-color full wraps should not be offered as the default 22 oz path.
- Handle tumblers should not be offered as full-color products unless Dana explicitly unlocks that decision.

## Inventory Strategy

Track the physical blank product and color inventory when Shopify's structure allows it cleanly.

The purpose is to create long-term production visibility and prevent overselling—not to create an unmanageable variant system.

Inventory choices should balance:

- actual blank stock
- decoration methods
- product colors
- manageable Shopify variants
- ease of future reporting

Do not treat every decoration choice as separate physical inventory if it consumes the same underlying blank.

# 9. Product Page Rules

Every product page must help the customer finish the order correctly.

## Required Product-Page Content

Where applicable:

- product name that humans and search engines understand
- clear price
- image gallery with uncropped products
- plain-language product description
- size and capacity
- available colors
- decoration options
- artwork requirements
- personalization field
- upload field
- special instructions
- proof explanation
- turnaround expectations
- pickup/shipping information
- care guidance
- related sizes or alternatives
- bulk-order path

## Option Design Rules

1. Use plain English.
2. Avoid internal production jargon.
3. Do not overwhelm customers with every possible exception.
4. Use conditional logic where possible.
5. Make required fields visibly required.
6. Explain why an upload or phone number is needed.
7. Prevent incomplete orders when the information is essential.
8. Keep choices easy to scan on mobile.

## Artwork Upload Guidance

Customers frequently provide low-quality graphics or files with backgrounds.

The website should teach customers that the best file is:

- a transparent PNG
- SVG, EPS, AI, or PDF vector when available
- high-resolution
- free of screenshots, textured backgrounds, or tiny unreadable details

The site should still provide a path for customers who do not know what file type they have. Do not shame them; explain what Beese & Co. can review and what may require artwork cleanup.

# 11. Beese Flash Artwork Standards

Beese Flash is the developing commercial illustration system for bookstore and bookish merchandise.

## Strategic Purpose

Create consistent, ownable, production-ready artwork that Beese & Co. can:

- engrave on tumblers
- adapt for bookstore buyers
- combine into collections
- customize for individual shop vibes
- reuse across products without looking generic

## Locked Production Style

Artwork intended for laser engraving and Illustrator Image Trace should use:

- black artwork only
- transparent background
- clean crisp edges
- bold but not excessively thick lines
- consistent line weight
- clear negative space
- limited interior detail
- no white background
- no grayscale
- no painterly texture
- no sketchy or scratchy lines
- no halftones
- no stippling unless specifically tested and approved
- no tiny fragile detail
- no overly realistic rendering
- minimal cleanup after Image Trace

Manufacturing quality comes before artistic realism.

## Collection Discipline

- Build one focused collection at a time.
- Preserve previously locked project standards.
- Do not overturn the workflow or reader-identity framework unless Dana explicitly unlocks it.
- Political and identity-based merchandising should remain neutral unless Dana explicitly chooses otherwise.

# 13. SEO Strategy — Major Priority

SEO is not a launch-afterthought. It must be built into every sprint.

## SEO Objectives

1. Help customers find Beese & Co. for the products and services they actually buy.
2. Build authority for custom tumblers, laser engraving, full-color drinkware, bulk orders, ornaments, golf products, and local custom gifts.
3. Capture both local and broader purchase-intent searches.
4. Create durable category and product pages rather than relying only on social traffic.

## Core SEO Rules

- One clear search intent per primary page.
- Unique page titles and meta descriptions.
- One useful H1 per page.
- Logical H2 and H3 hierarchy.
- Descriptive collection and product copy.
- Internal links between related product sizes, methods, and bulk options.
- Descriptive image alt text.
- Clean URL handles.
- No duplicate thin pages created merely for keywords.
- No keyword stuffing.
- Structured data must be valid and appropriate.
- Product availability, price, and review data must not be fabricated.
- Pages must be useful to a real customer first.

## Local SEO Direction

Use Spring Grove, Illinois and relevant service-area language naturally where it helps a customer understand pickup, local service, or delivery.

Do not stuff city names into every paragraph.

## Content Opportunities

Useful supporting content may include:

- how to prepare a logo for engraving
- transparent PNG versus screenshot
- laser engraving versus full-color printing
- how the proof process works
- choosing a tumbler size
- ordering logo tumblers for a business or bookstore
- care instructions
- bulk-order planning

Content should solve real customer questions and link directly to products.

# 14. Accessibility, Mobile, and Performance

These are production requirements, not polish.

## Mobile Rules

- Design and test every page for mobile first or mobile alongside desktop.
- Product choices must be easy to tap.
- Text fields and uploads must be usable on a phone.
- Sticky elements must not cover content.
- Menus must remain clear and predictable.
- Image galleries must not create extreme vertical length unnecessarily.
- Calls to action should remain visible without becoming intrusive.

## Accessibility Rules

- Maintain readable color contrast.
- Use semantic headings.
- Use descriptive labels for form inputs.
- Do not use placeholder text as the only label.
- Preserve visible keyboard focus.
- Use meaningful button and link text.
- Provide useful alt text.
- Avoid conveying required information by color alone.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.

## Performance Rules

- Compress and size images appropriately.
- Avoid loading oversized images on mobile.
- Minimize unnecessary apps and scripts.
- Avoid animation that delays shopping.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold media where appropriate.
- Keep theme JavaScript purposeful.
- Do not sacrifice usability for a minor performance score, but remove waste aggressively.

# 15. Shopify Engineering Rules

## Inspect Before Editing — Locked

Before changing code:

1. Inspect the existing implementation.
2. Identify what is working.
3. Preserve completed work unless there is a measurable reason to change it.
4. Reuse existing components when appropriate.
5. Avoid rebuilding an entire section to fix one issue.

## Theme Development Principles

- Build reusable sections and snippets.
- Keep merchant-editable content in theme settings when practical.
- Avoid hardcoding content that Dana will reasonably need to change.
- Use Shopify-native product, variant, collection, and metafield structures before adding apps.
- Add apps only when the business value clearly exceeds cost, complexity, and performance impact.
- Keep images easy to replace.
- Keep product templates flexible without exposing irrelevant settings.
- Preserve Shopify editor compatibility.
- Validate Liquid and schema changes.
- Do not leave dead settings, placeholder links, or unused code in production.

## Code Quality

- Name files and settings clearly.
- Comment only where the reason is not obvious.
- Avoid duplicated CSS and JavaScript.
- Scope component styles carefully.
- Avoid broad selectors that unintentionally alter Shopify or app elements.
- Preserve accessibility in custom controls.
- Test desktop and mobile.
- Check empty states.
- Check long product names and long option values.
- Check missing images.
- Check sold-out and unavailable variants.

## GitHub Discipline

- GitHub is the durable source for theme code and production documentation.
- Use clear branches and commits for meaningful work.
- Do not overwrite working production code blindly.
- Keep unrelated changes out of the same commit.
- Document major architecture and locked decisions.
- Prefer small, reviewable production changes over giant unexplained dumps.
- Keep a running ticket or sprint record.

Codex usage should be minimized unless it provides clear value. Do not burn tokens for work that the GitHub connector, direct code inspection, or normal editing can handle efficiently.

# 17. Decision and Collaboration Rules

## Source-of-Truth Rule

Previously locked Beese & Co. decisions remain locked until Dana explicitly unlocks them.

Do not re-litigate approved decisions merely because a new chat begins.

## Challenge Rule

The lead developer and creative partner is allowed—and expected—to challenge an idea when there is a stronger solution.

A challenge must:

- explain the business or customer impact
- offer a better path
- respect the brand and locked rules
- avoid changing things simply for personal taste

Agreement is not the goal. Building the strongest useful business is the goal.

## Proactive Work Rule

Proceed through logical production tasks without requiring Dana to approve every tiny step.

Stop only when:

- a decision materially changes the customer offer
- a price or product fact is unknown
- a locked rule must be changed
- production risk is significant
- Dana's visual preference is genuinely required

## No Drift Rule

Do not:

- revert to old navigation drafts
- add a Home icon
- reintroduce giant heroes
- crop critical product imagery
- invent products or prices
- create unnecessary clicks
- turn the site into a sign-shop catalog
- bury the proof process
- add trendy features without measurable value
- redo approved work without inspecting it

## Practical Communication Rule

Explain decisions clearly and directly.

Dana is learning GitHub and Shopify development but should not be talked down to. Use plain language, show what changed, and keep technical explanations tied to real outcomes.

# 20. The Final Filter

Before approving any website or business-system decision, ask:

1. Does this make it easier for the customer to order?
2. Does this collect better information upfront?
3. Does this reduce Dana's manual follow-up?
4. Does this protect production quality?
5. Does this support profitable growth?
6. Does this feel unmistakably like Beese & Co.?
7. Does this improve search visibility or customer trust?
8. Is it simpler than the alternative without becoming vague?
9. Can it scale without breaking the business?
10. Is it worth maintaining?

If the answer is no, remove it, simplify it, or redesign it.
